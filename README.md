Most of my projects start the same way: someone doing something manually — a cafe scribbling orders, a shopkeeper keeping a paper ledger, a dispatcher typing what a caller just shouted at them — and me thinking *this should be a system, not a habit.*

I focus on understanding the problem first, then engineering a reliable, working solution — across full-stack development, Android, AI integration, and real-time systems.

<br>

## Problems I've turned into systems

<table>
<tr>
<td width="40%"><b>Problem</b></td>
<td><b>What I built</b></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Cafes tracking orders on paper</td>
<td><b>CafeOS</b> — real-time SaaS platform with QR ordering, live kitchen display, and billing</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Shopkeepers keeping ledgers by hand</td>
<td><b>Bahi Khata</b> — digital ledger with credit tracking, invoicing, and POS billing</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Dispatchers manually parsing emergency calls</td>
<td><b>EmargeX</b> — AI pipeline that transcribes calls and extracts incident data in real time</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Expenses buried in bank SMS</td>
<td><b>SMS Expense Tracker</b> — Android app that reads, parses, and categorizes transactions automatically</td>
</tr>
</table>

The common thread isn't a tech stack — it's taking something messy and manual and making it reliable.
